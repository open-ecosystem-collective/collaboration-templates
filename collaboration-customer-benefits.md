# Why collaboration is good for the customer

**Arguments for bringing several partners into one project**

---

## TL;DR

- Customers worry that several providers will point at each other when something goes wrong. That worry is legitimate, and it can be solved.
- The answer is not one provider for everything. The answer is one lead, chosen by the customer, with the best specialists behind it.
- Done well, a team of specialists gives the customer more depth, more freedom and less dependency, while it still feels like working with one team.
- The benefits only hold if the partners prepare: agreements in writing, escalation defined before the first incident, and one face to the customer.

---

## The worry behind "one provider for everything"

Many customers choose a single provider not because it is better in every area, but because it feels safer. The fear is easy to understand: during an outage, three companies discuss whose fault it is while the customer waits in the middle. For customers in regulated industries, or customers who serve them, that delay is a business risk.

An example: a group of partners recently lost a tender with a joint offer that scored better on the technical side. The customer chose a provider offering everything from one hand. The partners had never explained how their collaboration would work, and the customer never asked. He simply chose the path that looked safer to him.

The lesson: the question "who is responsible when things are on fire?" belongs in the proposal, answered before anyone has to ask.

---

## The benefits for the customer

### 1. Depth in every area

Every company has its own strengths, people and values. A specialist knows its field better than a generalist knows all of them. In a team of specialists, each part of the project is handled by the people who know it best.

### 2. Still one point of contact

Several partners do not mean several phone numbers. One party leads the project and is the customer's single point of contact. The customer gets one commercial contact and one technical contact for the whole team. The coordination between the specialists happens out of sight.

### 3. The customer chooses the lead

The lead is not automatically the biggest company. It is the partner that fits the project and the customer best. Good partners are willing to work in someone else's team when another partner is the better fit.

### 4. Hand-offs become visible and agreed

A single large provider also passes tickets from team to team. The difference is that it happens inside one company, where the customer cannot see it. In a well-prepared partner team, the escalation path is written down before the project starts. The customer opens one ticket, and the hand-overs happen between the partners, along a path the customer knows.

### 5. No lock-in

In a team of specialists, each component can be replaced on its own. With one provider for everything, a change is only possible as a whole package, and every year of growth makes it more expensive.

### 6. Less concentration risk, more sovereignty

If one provider runs everything, every service shares that provider's problems. Spreading the work across specialists lowers the dependency on a single company. For organisations that take digital sovereignty seriously, this matters.

### 7. The right fit on a human level

Projects are run by people. A team of specialists can be assembled not only by skill, but also by how well the people fit the customer and each other.

### 8. A team that can grow with the project

When the customer's needs change, the team can change too. A new specialist can join for a new area without replacing everything that already works.

## One provider vs. a team of specialists

|                       | One provider for everything      | Team of specialists              |
|-----------------------|----------------------------------|----------------------------------|
| Depth per area        | Average across all areas         | One specialist per area          |
| Point of contact      | One, with many desks behind it   | One lead, with defined steering  |
| Escalation            | Internal, not visible            | Agreed and documented upfront    |
| Replaceability        | Only as a whole package          | Per component                    |
| Dependency            | High, on one company             | Low, sovereign                   |

---

## What it takes from the partners

The benefits above are not automatic. They hold when the partners do the following, early and in writing:

- **One lead, chosen by the customer**, not by company size.
- **A framework agreement between the partners**, agreed once, that sets the basis of the collaboration.
- **A short agreement per customer project** that names the lead, the contacts, the escalation paths, the responsibilities and the tasks.
- **One commercial contact and one technical contact** for the customer. Invoicing can be combined or separate, as agreed with the customer.
- **A shared incident runbook**, so escalation is defined before the first incident and not during it.
- **Showing up as one team.** Three companies standing next to each other in a pitch look like three companies. Rehearse before you pitch together.

None of this requires magic. It requires preparation, trust between the partners, and a bit of humility about who leads.

---

## Questions customers can ask a partner team

- Who is the lead, and is that in the contract?
- Who is my commercial contact, and who is my technical contact?
- What happens at 3 a.m. on a Sunday? Who picks up, and who does what next?
- Is the escalation path written down before the project starts?
- Can each component be replaced on its own if needed?
- Would the partners let someone else lead if that partner is the better fit?

A partner team that answers these questions clearly offers the customer the safety of one provider and the depth of many.

---

## Co-existence before collaboration

Not every company in an ecosystem has to be a close partner. It works in two steps:

1. **Co-existence:** no fighting, and supporting each other when it helps the customer.
2. **Collaboration:** when a real project needs it, with the agreements in writing.

Specialists beat the generalist. But only if the customer never has to manage the specialists.
