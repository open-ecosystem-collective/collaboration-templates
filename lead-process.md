# From lead to success story

**The lead process for a team of specialists, step by step**

---

## TL;DR

- Every step has one goal: the customer feels safe with a team of specialists, as if it were one provider.
- The partners prepare out of sight: roles, hours, escalation and price are settled before the customer meets the team.
- Passing a lead on to a better-placed partner is a step in the process, not a failure.
- The process ends with a success story, told only if every partner and the customer say yes.

---

## Before there is a lead: co-existence

Collaboration starts long before the first project.

- **Know the ecosystem.** Who does what, who is good at what, who fits which customers.
- **Sign the framework agreement at onboarding**, once, between two partners: [Collaboration Framework Agreement](templates/collaboration-framework-agreement.md).
- **Share the addendum for information** so both sides know what a project will look like: [Customer Project Addendum](templates/customer-project-addendum.md).
- **Co-exist:** no fighting, and support each other when it helps a customer.

## The steps in more detail

### 1 and 2 · Spot the lead, find the best fit

The first question is not «how do we win this?» but «who serves this customer best?». That can be you, a partner, or a team. If another partner is clearly better placed, pass the lead on (Tier 3, Art. 5). Saying «someone else is better at this» earns a fee and, more importantly, trust.

### 3 · Register the lead

Send a short email to the receiving partner's team address: prospect, main contact, the opportunity in two sentences and the tier you claim. The receiving partner confirms the tier in writing. Silence is not a yes.

### 4 · Form the team

Agree on the lead, ideally with the customer's view in mind: the lead is the partner that fits the project and the customer best, not the biggest company. Fill in the addendum before anyone talks money with the customer.

### 5 and 6 · Kick-off and incident runbook

Partners only, before any hours are booked. Settle the hour budget with an open buffer, the shared channel, the change process and the escalation path. Write the incident runbook now: who picks up, who does what next, how the customer opens one ticket. This is the answer to the customer's biggest worry.

### 7 and 8 · Pitch preparation and pitch

Answer «who is responsible when things are on fire?» in the proposal, before anyone asks. Bring at most three people: tech, sales and project or partner management. Rehearse. Three companies standing next to each other look like three companies; one rehearsed team looks like one team.

### 9 · Decision and contract

One contract with the customer, signed by the lead. The other partners work as subcontractors, or the partners sign a separate consortium agreement. That avoids an unintended simple partnership with joint and several liability (Art. 14.3).

### 10 · Delivery

Both SPOCs of every partner attend the coordination meetings. Changes go to everyone as soon as they are known. Nobody books hours beyond the budget before it has been aligned. New opportunities for the ecosystem are a fixed agenda item.

### 11 · Closing

Results against goals, final hours and settlement of fees and shares, a retrospective, and a clean hand-over to operations and support. Every component stays documented and replaceable: that's the customer's freedom.

### 12 · Success story

Ask every partner and the customer: do we tell this story? All or nothing. If yes, the [Open Ecosystem Success Stories](https://github.com/open-ecosystem-collective/stories) blueprint shows how, with the customer first.

---

## When the lead is not for you

Not every lead needs a team. Sometimes the best answer is to pass it on completely: register it as Tier 3, introduce the customer to the better-placed partner and step back. The customer gets the right specialist, the partner gets the work, and you get the trust that brings the next lead back to you.
