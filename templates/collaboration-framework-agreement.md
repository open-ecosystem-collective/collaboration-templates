## About this template

This template gives two companies a ready-made framework to work together on customer projects instead of competing for them. It is one of two templates that belong together: the **Collaboration Framework Agreement** (the basis of the collaboration, signed once) and the **Customer Project Addendum** (the «mission report» for each joint customer project).

The Addendum is a separate document: [OEC Template: Customer Project Addendum](customer-project-addendum.md).

The template is fully anonymised and symmetrical: both parties have the same rights and obligations, and either one can refer, receive or take the lead.

**How to use it**

1. Replace every placeholder in `[square brackets]` with your details.
2. Where a clause carries an **OEC recommendation**, take the suggested option or choose an alternative.
3. Keep or delete the clauses marked *optional*, then check the numbering.
4. Delete all recommendation blocks (the quote blocks) before signing.
5. Have the draft reviewed by a lawyer.

## Principles of the collaboration

The agreement puts one stance into practice: co-existence and collaboration instead of competition. Together, the specialists beat the generalist.

- **The customer is at the centre.** Not one company, but the customer's needs decide who does what.
- **On the same level.** Both parties are equals. Either can take the lead, and the customer has a say in who leads.
- **Everyone does what they do best.** When capacity or expertise is missing, the opportunity goes to the right partner instead of being kept at any cost.
- **Mutual.** All fees, obligations and protection clauses apply in both directions.
- **One point of contact for the customer.** Each project has one contract, one commercial contact and one technical contact. Separate invoices remain possible.
- **Fairness over control.** The agreement covers what is necessary and otherwise relies on each party's self-accountability.

### I. Subject Matter and Scope

**Art. 1 Purpose and Scope**

1.1 This Agreement sets out the terms under which the Parties collaborate on joint customer projects, lead referrals, co-selling activities, shared delivery and joint or bundled solutions.

1.2 This Agreement is a framework. The specific terms of each project are set out in a separate Customer Project Addendum (the «Addendum», Annex 1), which becomes an integral part of this Agreement once signed by both Parties.

1.3 In the event of a conflict between this Agreement and an Addendum, the Addendum prevails for the project it relates to.

1.4 This Agreement is non-exclusive. Each Party remains free to collaborate with other partners, including competitors of the other Party, and to serve customers directly. No exclusivity arises from this Agreement unless it is expressly agreed in an Addendum for a specific project.

1.5 **Multi-party projects.** This Agreement is always concluded between two Parties. An Addendum for a joint project may involve any number of companies, provided each of them has a Collaboration Framework Agreement with at least one other company in the project. Between companies in the same project that have no direct agreement with each other, the terms of this Agreement apply accordingly for that project only.

> **OEC recommendation:** Keep the Framework Agreement bilateral: it stays simple to sign and to end. Larger constellations (for example infrastructure, platform and consulting partner) come together per project in one Addendum. For companies that only contribute a small part, listing them as subcontractors (Art. 3.4) is often simpler.

> **OEC recommendation:** Sign the Framework Agreement at onboarding and share the Addendum for information only. Sign the Addendum per lead or project, ideally only for active collaboration (Tier 1).

**Art. 2 Commencement and Term**

2.1 This Agreement enters into force when signed by both Parties and runs for an indefinite period.

2.2 Either Party may terminate this Agreement in writing with [three (3)] months' notice to the end of a calendar month, provided that all active Addenda have been completed or terminated separately.

2.3 The right to terminate without notice for good cause remains reserved. Good cause includes in particular a material breach of this Agreement not remedied within [thirty (30)] days of written notice, insolvency, or assignment of this Agreement without the other Party's written consent.

2.4 Termination does not affect Addenda that are still active; this Agreement continues to apply to them until they are completed. Referral fees and revenue shares that arose before termination, or that arise from customer contracts signed before termination, remain due under Art. 5 and Art. 7. The provisions on confidentiality (Art. 9), intellectual property (Art. 10), fair play (Art. 6), non-solicitation (Art. 12), liability (Art. 13) and governing law (Art. 20) survive termination.

> **OEC recommendation:** Three months' notice is a good middle ground. Shorter feels non-committal, longer puts off new partners.

### II. Roles and Responsibilities

**Art. 3 Lead Partner**

3.1 For each joint project, one Party is designated as Lead Partner in the relevant Addendum. The Lead Partner is the customer's primary point of contact (SPOC), has overall responsibility for the project, coordinates the invoicing of the end customer according to the billing model chosen in the Addendum (Art. 7.2) and, unless the Addendum states otherwise, provides first-level support.

3.2 Either Party may be Lead Partner. The choice follows the customer's needs and the focus of the project; the customer's preference is taken into account.

> **OEC recommendation:** Art. 3.2 is new compared with many partner agreements. It takes the question «who leads?» out of the power play and hands it back to the customer. In tenders, it answers the objection «too many parties involved»: one Lead Partner steers the others, and the customer has one contact.

3.3 Roles may be adjusted per project in the Addendum. Any change to the designation of the Lead Partner requires the written consent of both Parties.

3.4 **Subcontractors.** Each Party may, and is encouraged to, involve subcontractors in joint projects, in particular further ecosystem partners with the relevant expertise. The Party involving a subcontractor informs the other Party and, where the customer contract requires it, the customer in advance, and lists the subcontractor in the Addendum. It remains responsible to the other Party for the work of its subcontractors and passes on the obligations of this Agreement on confidentiality (Art. 9), fair play (Art. 6), non-solicitation (Art. 12) and data protection (Art. 16) to them.

> **OEC recommendation:** Subcontracting is how an ecosystem grows: the more specialists work on a project, the better the solution for the customer. Where a subcontractor is itself the right fit for a lead of its own, a separate Framework Agreement with it is the next step.

**Art. 4 Contacts and Communication**

4.1 Each Party appoints a responsible contact person (Partner Manager) for the duration of the collaboration. Changes of contact persons must be notified in writing without delay.

4.2 Formal notices and legally relevant declarations are sent to the other Party's registered business address or to the email address of its designated contact person.

4.3 **Sharing changes.** Any change to a customer agreement, scope, timeline, budget or contact that affects a joint project is shared with all parties involved in that project, including subcontractors, as soon as it is known, and no later than [two (2)] working days after it becomes known.

4.4 **SPOCs in every meeting.** For each joint project, each Party names a Sales SPOC and a Technical SPOC in the Addendum. Both attend every project meeting (kick-off, pitch preparation, coordination and closing meetings), so that all information is available to all Parties at the same time. Not every Party has to attend every meeting: a Party that cannot attend may send a briefed deputy or hand its attendance to another partner in the project whom it fully trusts. That partner represents it in the meeting and passes on all information without delay.

4.5 **Small team towards the customer.** In meetings with the customer, including the pitch, the Parties are represented by at most three people in total, ideally one technical expert, one sales person and one person from project or partner management. The Lead Partner coordinates who attends. All SPOCs are briefed before and informed after every customer meeting.

> **OEC recommendation:** However many companies work on the project, the customer should see one team. Three people with clear roles show one team and answer the objection «too many parties» before it is raised.

> **OEC recommendation:** Use a team address (e.g. partner@ or sales@) for lead registrations rather than a personal one. It stays valid when people change roles.

### III. Commercial Principles

**Art. 5 Lead Referral Fees**

5.1 Where one Party refers a qualified lead to the other Party, a referral fee is due according to the tier structure below. All three tiers apply in both directions: either Party can be the referring or the receiving Party.

5.2 All referral fees are calculated on the final contracted project value, excluding VAT, third-party licence or subscription costs and pass-through costs. The fee is due only if the referral leads to a signed customer contract.

5.3 Fees are due and payable within [thirty (30)] days after the receiving Party has received full payment from the end customer.

5.4 A lead must be registered within [five (5)] working days of the first introduction by email to the receiving Party's address for lead registrations (Art. 4.1), stating: prospect name, primary contact, a short description of the opportunity and the claimed tier. The receiving Party confirms the applicable tier in writing within [five (5)] working days. Silence does not constitute consent. Lead registrations are handled on a first-come, first-served basis unless otherwise agreed in writing.

5.5 Referral fees apply only to the originally contracted scope. Renewals carry no referral fee. Extensions, expansions or follow-on orders require a separate written agreement.

5.6 Referral fees apply only to revenue contracted and invoiced within [twelve (12)] months of the first effective date of the relevant customer contract, unless otherwise agreed in writing.

5.7 **Transparency.** The receiving Party informs the referring Party in writing of the contracted project value within [ten (10)] working days of signing the customer contract, and of the customer payments received, so that the referring Party can verify its fee. On request, the receiving Party provides the relevant extracts of invoices and payment records.

5.8 **Invoicing and VAT.** The referring Party invoices the referral fee to the receiving Party once the fee is due (Art. 5.3). Referral fees are stated excluding VAT; VAT is added at the applicable statutory rate where due.

| Tier | Fee | Qualifies when … | Does not qualify when … | Decision test |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Tier 1 Collaboration** | [10] % | The referring Party provides the lead AND actively co-sells: joins calls or demos, co-writes proposals, contributes to scoping, advocates internally or is named in the contract. Requires two or more qualifying activities. | Introduction only, with no documented involvement in the sales cycle. | *Would the deal have been harder without them? Yes → Tier 1.* |
| **Tier 2 Warm Intro** | [5] % | The referring Party makes a qualified introduction (name, contact, use case) and is not involved beyond that. | The referring Party joins a follow-up meeting or call, or contributes to a document after the introduction. | *Any involvement after the introduction? No → Tier 2.* |
| **Tier 3 Pass On** | [3] % | A Party passes an opportunity to the other Party because it lacks capacity or expertise, or is not best placed to serve the customer. It has no active sales or delivery role. | The referring Party takes an active sales or delivery role. In that case, see Tier 1 or 2. | *Does the referring Party step back completely, with no sales or delivery role? Yes → Tier 3.* |

> **OEC recommendation:** Name the tiers by the level of involvement, not by lead quality («cold», «warm»). That avoids arguments about how good a lead was. The rates 10 / 5 / 3 % are a suggested starting point; keep them the same in both directions so neither side feels short-changed. Tier 3 is the heart of co-existence: it rewards a partner for saying «someone else is better placed for this».

**Art. 6 Fair Play**

6.1 Neither Party may deliberately bypass the other Party's commercial involvement in a referred engagement or in a customer project governed by an Addendum, during the active term of that project.

6.2 Neither Party may undercut the other within this collaboration. Undercutting means approaching a customer or prospect introduced or referred by the other Party directly, or offering it services at lower prices or on materially more favourable terms, in a way reasonably likely to displace the other Party. It includes in particular: (a) bidding on a registered or referred opportunity without the referring Party's knowledge; (b) offering equivalent services to a joint customer at a lower rate to displace the other Party; and (c) using knowledge of the other Party's pricing, scope or offer to compete against it.

6.3 Articles 6.1 and 6.2 do not apply where: (a) the Parties compete in the general market and no referral or introduction under this Agreement has taken place; (b) a bid is lost and a Party rebids or responds to an RFI/RFP, in which case another partner may be involved after prior written notice to the other Party; (c) the end customer explicitly requests a different partner in writing, in which case the Party concerned documents the request and informs the other Party without delay; or (d) several offers with different partners are submitted in response to the same open RFI/RFP and no exclusivity has been established.

> **OEC recommendation:** One article instead of three keeps the rule easy to remember: if you would not do it openly in front of your partner, do not do it.

**Art. 7 Project Revenue Split**

7.1 The total project price is agreed in writing between the Parties before a customer offer is submitted. The revenue split reflects each Party's contribution in terms of effort, delivery responsibility, commercial responsibility, project management, risk and customer relationship management. Guideline values for the split may be agreed in the relevant Addendum.

7.2 Two billing models are available and are selected in the Addendum:

- **Direct invoicing:** each Party invoices its own share directly to the end customer and bears its own payment risk.
- **Single invoice with redistribution:** one Party invoices the full amount and passes on the other Party's share. The invoicing Party bears the risk of non-payment by the end customer. An obligation to pay the other Party arises only once and only if payment from the end customer has actually been received. Redistribution takes place within the number of days stated in the Addendum, and no later than [thirty (30)] days after receipt of the customer payment.

7.3 Prices are aligned between the Parties before every customer offer. Discounts require the written consent of both Parties. Discounts requested by the customer after the offer is submitted are allocated pro rata unless otherwise agreed.

> **OEC recommendation:** Promise the customer one contract and one contact, but never automatically «one invoice». Direct invoicing is often simpler, because each Party keeps its own payment risk.

7.4 **Joint package.** The Parties may offer customers a combined solution of their services as one package at one price. Package content, price, revenue split and Lead Partner are set out in the Addendum; Art. 7.1 to 7.3 apply accordingly.

7.5 **Hour budgets and buffer.** Hour budgets are always planned with sufficient buffer for risks and coordination [e.g. at least 15 %] and are stated in the Addendum. No Party books or commits hours beyond the agreed budget before this has been aligned with the other parties in a project meeting. A foreseeable overrun is shared as soon as it is known (Art. 4.3).

> **OEC recommendation:** Overbooked hours are the most common reason joint projects turn sour. Plan the buffer openly in the Addendum instead of hiding it in each Party's estimate, so everyone sees the same number.

7.6 **Own costs for collaboration.** Each Party bears its own costs for the collaboration itself. This includes in particular coordination, kick-off and closing meetings, partner management, lead registration and referrals, presales and joint offer preparation, account mapping, enablement and joint marketing. This effort is not invoiced to the other Party, is not offset against fees or revenue shares, and is not counted against the project hour budget (Art. 7.5). Only the delivery services agreed in the Addendum are billable.

### IV. Confidentiality and Intellectual Property

**Art. 9 Confidentiality**

*Choose option A or B and delete the other.*

9.1 **Option A (existing NDA):** Confidentiality between the Parties is governed exclusively by the [name of NDA] dated [date] [available at: link], which applies to this Agreement and to every Addendum. In the event of a conflict on confidentiality matters, the NDA prevails.

9.1 **Option B (built-in clause):** Each Party keeps confidential all non-public information of the other Party that it receives in connection with this Agreement, in particular on customers, prices, offers and technology, and uses it only for the purposes of this collaboration. This obligation does not apply to information that is or becomes publicly known without breach, was already known to the receiving Party, was independently developed, or must be disclosed by law or by order of an authority. The obligation continues for [three (3)] years after termination of this Agreement.

> **OEC recommendation:** If one Party already has a published NDA, option A saves a second negotiation. With partners of very different size, option B is fairer, because neither side signs the other's paper.

**Art. 10 Intellectual Property**

10.1 Each Party retains all rights to its pre-existing intellectual property. Nothing in this Agreement constitutes a transfer of intellectual property rights.

10.2 Intellectual property created jointly or specifically for a customer project is defined and allocated in the relevant Addendum.

> **OEC recommendation:** For open source work, state in the Addendum under which licence joint results are published. That makes reuse in the ecosystem possible from the start.

**Art. 11 Partner Branding**

11.1 Either Party may name the other Party as a collaboration partner on its website or in its marketing materials, subject to the other Party's prior written approval.

11.2 Before publication, each Party submits the intended mention or use of the other Party's brand materials for review and approval. The reviewing Party responds within [ten (10)] working days of receipt. Silence after this period is deemed approval.

11.3 Neither Party may use the other Party's logo, brand or name in a way that misrepresents the relationship, disparages the other Party's brand or breaches its written brand guidelines.

11.4 On termination of this Agreement, each Party stops using the other Party's brand materials within [thirty (30)] days unless otherwise agreed in writing.

> **OEC recommendation:** Do not publish which partners have signed the agreement. Say only that a framework exists and that the path is taken together when a project comes up. Joint success stories need the approval of every party involved: all or nothing.

### V. Customer Protection

**Art. 12 Non-Solicitation and Existing Customers**

12.1 Neither Party may solicit or attempt to solicit employees, contractors or key persons of the other Party who are or were involved in a project under this Agreement, during the term of this Agreement and for [twelve (12)] months thereafter. This clause applies mutually and takes precedence over any deviating provision in either Party's general terms and conditions.

12.2 Existing customer relationships of each Party are respected. Neither Party may use information about the other Party's customers obtained through this collaboration to acquire those customers independently.

12.3 *Optional:* In the event of a breach of Art. 12.1, a contractual penalty of [twelve (12)] gross monthly salaries of the person concerned is due.

> **OEC recommendation:** The penalty in Art. 12.3 should hurt, but stay proportionate: under Swiss law a court must reduce an excessive contractual penalty (Art. 163 para. 3 CO). With small partners, [six (6)] months can feel fairer. A non-solicitation clause between companies is generally permissible within the limits of Art. 27 of the Swiss Civil Code. Applications initiated by the employee on their own (e.g. via a public job ad) are often excluded explicitly.

### VI. Liability

**Art. 13 Liability**

13.1 Each Party is liable to the other for damage caused intentionally or through gross negligence. Liability for damage caused by slight negligence and for damage caused by auxiliary persons is excluded to the extent permitted by law.

13.2 Neither Party is liable for indirect damage, loss of profit or consequential damage, to the extent permitted by law. Neither Party is liable for failure or delay caused by events beyond its reasonable control (force majeure).

13.3 *Optional:* Subject to the limitations in this Article 13, each Party indemnifies the other against direct damage resulting from its own breach of this Agreement or from the infringement of third-party rights, including reasonable legal costs.

13.4 *Optional:* The total liability of each Party under this Agreement is limited to [the fees received under the relevant Addendum in the last twelve (12) months / CHF …].

> **OEC recommendation:** Many partner agreements simply refer to one party's general terms and conditions. In this template the liability clause stands on its own, so that neither Party has to accept the other's terms. Art. 13.4 is optional and useful when project volumes are high.

### VII. Review and Escalation

**Art. 14 Governance**

14.1 The Parties hold a partnership review at least [once a year] to assess the collaboration, address open points and agree on improvements.

14.2 Each Party names an escalation contact, stated in the Addendum. Disputes are first addressed between the escalation contacts within [ten (10)] working days before formal proceedings are initiated.

14.3 Nothing in this Agreement creates a simple partnership (einfache Gesellschaft), joint venture, agency, fiduciary relationship, employment relationship or any other legal association between the Parties. Neither Party may bind or represent the other unless expressly agreed in writing.

> **OEC recommendation:** A quarterly sync works better than the annual minimum: pipeline, joint activities, success story candidates. Report at partner level, never per person. Project reviews and technical procedures (tickets, support) belong in a separate technical runbook between the technical SPOCs. Note on Art. 14.3: if the Parties submit a joint bid and sign one contract with the customer together, Swiss law usually treats them as a simple partnership (Art. 530 ff. CO) with joint and several liability towards the customer, whatever this clause says. To avoid this, let the Lead Partner contract with the customer and involve the other Party as subcontractor, or conclude a separate consortium agreement.

### VIII. General Provisions

**Art. 15 Amendments, Form and Signature**

15.1 Amendments to this Agreement must be in writing and signed by authorised representatives of both Parties. This also applies to any waiver of this written form requirement.

15.2 This Agreement and every Addendum may be signed electronically, using a qualified electronic signature or an electronic signature solution agreed between the Parties (e.g. [Skribble, DocuSign, Odoo Sign, Adobe Sign]). An electronic signature has the same effect as a handwritten one between the Parties.

15.3 This Agreement may be signed in counterparts, each of which is deemed an original; together they form one and the same document.

> **OEC recommendation:** Under Art. 14 para. 2bis CO, only a qualified electronic signature (QES) combined with a qualified time stamp is equivalent to a handwritten signature. Because Art. 15.1 agrees on written form, Art. 15.2 is needed to allow other e-signature solutions as well. The law prescribes no form for this type of agreement, so the Parties are free to agree on this.

**Art. 16 Data Protection and Compliance**

16.1 Each Party complies with applicable data protection law in performing this Agreement and every Addendum. Where required by law or by a customer contract, the Parties conclude separate data processing or data sharing agreements defining their roles and responsibilities.

16.2 Each Party complies with applicable anti-corruption, sanctions and export control laws in connection with this Agreement. Neither Party offers or accepts undue advantages to obtain or keep business under this Agreement.

**Art. 17 Assignment**

17.1 Neither Party may assign its rights or obligations under this Agreement to a third party without the other Party's prior written consent. This clause takes precedence over any deviating provision in either Party's general terms and conditions.

**Art. 18 Severability**

18.1 If any provision of this Agreement is or becomes invalid, ineffective or unenforceable, it is replaced by a valid provision that comes closest to the economic purpose of the original. The validity of the remaining provisions is not affected.

**Art. 19 Entire Agreement**

19.1 This Agreement, together with its Addenda, constitutes the entire agreement between the Parties on its subject matter and supersedes all prior negotiations, representations and agreements, whether oral or written.

**Art. 20 Governing Law and Jurisdiction**

20.1 This Agreement is governed exclusively by substantive [Swiss] law, excluding its conflict-of-law rules and the UN Convention on Contracts for the International Sale of Goods (CISG).

20.2 The exclusive place of jurisdiction for all disputes arising from or in connection with this Agreement is [City, Country].

20.3 In the event of discrepancies between language versions of this Agreement, the [English / German] version prevails.

> **OEC recommendation:** Choose the seat of the Party that drafted the agreement, or a neutral place between both. If the Agreement exists in two languages, decide in Art. 20.3 which one is binding before sending it out.
