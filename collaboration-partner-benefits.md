# Why collaboration is good for the partner

**What a company gains from co-existence and collaboration in an ecosystem**

---

## TL;DR

- You don't have to be good at everything. You focus on what you do best, and the ecosystem covers the rest.
- Together, specialists win projects that none of them would win alone, including against providers who offer everything from one hand.
- Passing a lead on is rewarded, small partners can lead, and nobody is locked in.
- It costs preparation, openness and a bit of humility. It doesn't cost money to start: the templates are open.

---

## The benefits for the partner

### 1. Focus on what you do best

Every company has its own strengths, people and values. You no longer have to stretch into areas where others are better, just to keep a customer. You do your part well and leave the rest to the specialists who know it best.

### 2. Win projects you couldn't win alone

A customer who needs several specialists either takes one generalist or a well-prepared team. With agreements in writing and one face to the customer, your team can answer the question that loses tenders: «who is responsible when things are on fire?».

### 3. Saying no earns trust and a fee

When you lack capacity or expertise, passing the lead to a better-placed partner is rewarded (Tier 3 Pass On). The customer remembers who sent them to the right place, and the partner remembers who sent the work. Both come back.

### 4. On the same level, whatever your size

The lead isn't automatically the biggest company. It's the partner that fits the project and the customer best. A small specialist can lead, and a large one can work in someone else's team without losing face.

### 5. Clear rules instead of mistrust

Lead registration, referral tiers, fair play and transparency on fees are agreed once, in writing. Nobody has to wonder whether a partner will go around them. If you wouldn't do it openly in front of your partner, you don't do it.

### 6. No surprises in the budget

Hour budgets include an open buffer, nobody books beyond the budget without alignment, and every partner bears its own collaboration effort. Nobody sends an invoice for a coordination meeting.

### 7. No lock-in for you either

The framework agreement is non-exclusive, bilateral and can be ended with three months' notice. You stay free to work with anyone, including competitors of your partners.

### 8. Capacity that breathes

When a project grows, a specialist joins as subcontractor. When it shrinks, nobody carries staff they don't need. The ecosystem is your bench.

### 9. Reach that multiplies

A joint success story is carried by every partner through its own network. Your name reaches rooms you would never enter alone, without a marketing budget.

### 10. Work with people you like

Projects are run by people. In an ecosystem you choose partners not only by skill, but by how well you work together. That makes the hard days easier.

## Going it alone or working in an ecosystem

|                        | Going it alone                         | Working in an ecosystem                     |
|------------------------|----------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------|
| Scope you can offer    | Only what you cover yourself           | What the team covers                        |
| Leads you can't serve  | Lost, or served badly                  | Passed on, and rewarded                     |
| Tenders against generalists | Hard to win                       | Winnable as one prepared team               |
| Capacity peaks         | Overtime or saying no                  | A specialist joins                          |
| Visibility             | Your own channels                      | Every partner's channels                    |

---

## What it takes from you

- **Openness.** Share changes, hours and opportunities as soon as you know them.
- **Preparation.** Agreements, escalation and the incident runbook are settled before the customer meets the team.
- **Humility about who leads.** Sometimes the best move is to stand in someone else's team, or to step back completely.
- **Discipline with hours.** Plan the buffer openly and stick to the budget.
- **Self-accountability.** The agreements cover what's necessary. The rest relies on you.

---

## How to start

1. Read the [lead process](lead-process.md) and see if it fits how you want to work.
2. Talk to a company you already respect and co-exist with.
3. Sign the [Collaboration Framework Agreement](templates/collaboration-framework-agreement.md) with them, after a legal review.
4. When the first lead comes up, use the [Customer Project Addendum](templates/customer-project-addendum.md) and the [meeting notes](templates/coordination-meeting-notes.md).

Specialists beat the generalist. Together, and only if the customer never has to manage them.
